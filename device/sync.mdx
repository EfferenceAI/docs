---
title: "Multi-Camera Sync"
description: "Synchronize the clocks and frames of two or more M1 cameras"
---

Two or more M1 cameras can work as one **synced group**. One camera is the
**leader**: its clock is the group's timebase. The others are **followers**:
each one disciplines its clock to the leader's, camera to camera, with no
computer or phone in the loop once the group is formed.

When a camera's clock is locked, it also aligns the timing of its frames to the
same shared schedule, so frames from every camera in the group are captured at
the same instant. Recordings and live streams from a synced group therefore
pair frame for frame, and their timestamps are directly comparable.

## Requirements

- Two or more M1 cameras running firmware with multi-camera sync (see the
  [firmware changelog](/changelog/firmware)).
- Every camera joined to the **same WiFi network** (see
  [WiFi Provisioning](/device/wifi)). The group uses it for downloads; the sync
  itself runs on a separate link, described below.
- A phone with the Efference app, or a computer with `ef-cli`, that can reach
  every camera over Bluetooth LE (or USB, for `ef-cli`).
- Frame alignment requires the cameras to capture at **30 fps**. At other
  frame rates the clocks still synchronize, but frames are not aligned.

## How it works

Forming a group does the following, in order:

1. The phone or computer sets every camera's clock from its own, the leader's
   first, so the group starts close to real time. See
   [Time of day](#time-of-day-utc) for when that time counts as confirmed UTC.
2. The leader starts a private WPA3 network, the **sync link**, on the same
   channel as its WiFi uplink, with a key it generates itself. The key is
   renewed every time the group is formed.
3. Each follower receives the link's credentials, joins it, and starts
   following the leader's clock. Followers keep their normal WiFi connection
   for uploads and downloads.
4. The group is formed once every camera reports **LOCKED** and holds the same
   link key.

A leader's sync link holds up to 31 followers.

<Warning>
Use a WiFi network on a non-DFS channel (36–48 or 149–165 in the 5 GHz band). On a DFS channel
(52–144) the leader cannot host the sync link on the uplink's channel and picks another one. Each
camera has a single radio, so it then switches between the two channels: it spends about 55–60 ms
of every 100 ms away from the WiFi network, which roughly halves WiFi throughput and drops packets
from live streams.
</Warning>

The group survives reboots: a camera that restarts rejoins its group and
locks again on its own.

## Form a group in the Efference app

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the cameras">
    Open the **Group** tab and tap **Scan for cameras**. Tick every camera that
    should be in the group. The first camera you pick is the leader; tap
    **Make leader** on another camera to change it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect">
    Tap **Connect N cameras**. The app opens a Bluetooth connection to each one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Form the group">
    Tap **Form group**. The app sets the clocks, starts the sync link on the
    leader, and waits until every camera is locked. This usually takes under
    15 seconds.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Leave **Clock filter** on **Kalman** unless Efference support advises
otherwise.

### Read the sync lights

Every camera in the Group tab, and every tile in the group live view, shows
two lights:

| Light | Green means | Red means |
|---|---|---|
| **Clock sync** | The camera's clock is locked to the leader. The leader shows green while it serves the group. | Not locked yet, lock lost, or the camera is not answering. |
| **Phase lock** | The camera's frames are aligned with the rest of the group. | The camera is not recording or streaming, is not at 30 fps, or has not aligned yet. |

The lights refresh every two seconds while the Group tab is open. **Phase
lock** is red whenever a camera is idle; that is expected, because frames are
only aligned while the camera captures. Hold a finger on a light to see why
it is red.

A **UTC unreliable · cameras synced to each other** pill appears when the group's time of day is
not confirmed. The cameras are still synced to each other and record as usual; only the absolute
time on the recordings may be off. Tap the pill to see why. See
[Time of day](#time-of-day-utc).

### Record, view, and download

- **Record on all cameras** starts a recording with the same name on every
  camera. A follower whose clock is not locked refuses to record, so a group
  recording is always synchronized. Recording does not depend on the time of
  day being confirmed. **Stop recording** stops all of them.
- **Live view (all cameras)** streams every camera side by side, each with its
  frame offset from the first camera in microseconds.
- **Download … from all cameras** copies each camera's recording to the phone
  over WiFi, into one folder per camera. The phone must be on the same WiFi
  network as the cameras. A camera that cannot send over WiFi falls back to
  Bluetooth, which is much slower.

### Leave a group

- **Disconnect (keep the group)** closes the app's connections. The cameras
  stay synced and keep their group across reboots.
- **Return every camera to standalone** turns sync off on every camera and
  shuts down the sync link, erasing its key.

## Form a group with ef-cli

Name every camera with `--ble <MAC>` or `--serial <serial>`, then run `init`.
The first camera named is the leader:

```bash
ef-cli --ble 74:A2:35:10:00:19 --ble 60:48:9C:BA:1D:95 init
```

```text
[M1-39] leader  LOCKED  key 08f137a69d86c69e  locked in 0.0 s
[M1-462cad6f133818a7] follower  LOCKED  key 08f137a69d86c69e  locked in 2.1 s
group formed: recordings and streams from these cameras are phase-aligned
```

Every camera reports the same `key`: a fingerprint of the sync link's key, which
itself is never shown. Options:

| Option | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `--leader <MAC\|serial>` | Choose the leader instead of the first camera named. |
| `--timeout <s>` | How long to wait for every camera to lock (default 60 s). |

Any other command runs on each camera of the group in turn, with its output
prefixed by the camera:

```bash
ef-cli --ble 74:A2:35:10:00:19 --ble 60:48:9C:BA:1D:95 record start take-1
ef-cli --ble 74:A2:35:10:00:19 --ble 60:48:9C:BA:1D:95 record stop
ef-cli --ble 74:A2:35:10:00:19 --ble 60:48:9C:BA:1D:95 download take-1 ./takes
```

`download` saves each camera's copy under `./takes/<camera>/`.

### Check the sync state

`info` shows each camera's role, its lock state, its offset from the leader,
whether its frames are aligned, and whether its time of day is confirmed UTC:

```text
[39] sync             : leader, LOCKED, serving 1 follower
[39] frame phase      : locked, error -34.4 us
[39] utc              : reliable (time source ble)
[462cad6f133818a7] sync             : follower, LOCKED, offset +11.1 us +- 33.5 us, locked in 2.1 s
[462cad6f133818a7] frame phase      : locked, error +7.7 us
[462cad6f133818a7] utc              : from the leader (time source sync)
```

`frame phase` reads `off` while a camera is idle, `acquiring` while it aligns
its frames after a recording or stream starts, and `locked` once they are
aligned. `info --json` carries the same fields for scripts.

To return a camera to standalone, run `init` with that camera alone:

```bash
ef-cli --ble 60:48:9C:BA:1D:95 init
```

## Lock states

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ACQUIRING` | The follower is measuring the leader's clock. |
| `LOCKED` | The clock follows the leader. Recording is allowed. |
| `HOLDOVER` | The leader went quiet; the follower keeps time on its own for a short while. |
| `DEGRADED` | The link to the leader is too noisy for full accuracy. |
| `LOST` | The follower has no timebase. It refuses to record until it locks again. |

### What `LOCKED` means

A follower reports `LOCKED` only while its estimated offset from the leader, plus twice the
estimate's uncertainty, is under 200 µs. The uncertainty includes a 15 µs allowance for link
error that a two-way exchange cannot see. Measured against an LED timing panel on two cameras,
frames from a locked group were within 56–76 µs of each other at the 99th percentile.

Three limits apply:
- **The link is assumed symmetric.** Time over Wi-Fi is measured from round trips, and a delay
  that affects only one direction is invisible to any round-trip method. A slowly changing
  one-way delay moves the true offset without moving the estimate.
- **A sudden change in the leader's clock rate shows up at the next measurement.** A follower
  measures every 10 s or more often, so a sudden 20 ppm change in the leader's rate can read
  `LOCKED` while more than 200 µs off for up to about 15 s. The leader's own rate corrections are
  announced to followers and do not cause this.
- **After a short outage.** A follower keeps `LOCKED` for up to 30 s without hearing the leader,
  then goes to `HOLDOVER`.

## Time of day (UTC)

Synchronization and the time of day are separate. The cameras of a group always share the leader's
timebase, so their timestamps pair with each other whatever time the leader holds. Whether that time
is also correct UTC depends on where the leader's clock came from:

| Time source | UTC | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `usb`, `ble` | Reliable | Set by a phone or computer whose own clock is confirmed against internet time (NTP) |
| `mtls` | Reliable | The camera's own network time |
| `rtc` | Reliable | The camera's battery-backed clock, last set from one of the above |
| `usb_unsynced`, `ble_unsynced`, `rtc_unsynced` | Unreliable | Set by a phone or computer whose clock is not confirmed, or kept from such a set |
| `none` | Unreliable | Not set since the camera started, and no kept time |
| `sync`, `sync-untrusted` | The leader's | A follower takes its time from the leader |

A computer counts as confirmed when its operating system reports the clock synchronized by NTP
(chrony or systemd-timesyncd), the clock's error bound is under 2 seconds, and, after a sleep, the
NTP daemon has updated the clock since the computer woke.

When the time is unreliable the cameras still record, and every recording carries its time source
in its metadata (`clock.source`). To get confirmed UTC, connect from a phone or computer whose
clock is synchronized and form the group again.

## When sync is lost

- **The leader restarts or loses power.** Followers go to `HOLDOVER`, then
  `LOST`. When the leader comes back, they rejoin and lock again on their own,
  usually within a minute. A recording that was already running keeps
  running, but frames it captures while a camera is not `LOCKED` are not
  guaranteed to be aligned; a new recording waits for the lock.
- **A follower is out of range of the leader.** It cannot hear the sync link
  and drops out of `LOCKED`. Move it closer; it relocks on its own.
- **A camera does not lock after forming.** Check that every camera is on the
  same WiFi network, then form the group again. Forming again always issues a
  new link key.

<Note>
Setting a follower's clock by hand while it is locked has no effect: the group's
timebase wins. Once the leader serves followers, a phone or computer connecting
later moves the group's clock only when its own clock is confirmed UTC, the
difference is over 250 ms, and no camera is recording, and at most once every
10 minutes. Smaller differences only correct the leader's rate. To reset the
group's time at once, form the group again.
</Note>

## Use sync from the SDK

`ef::Group` forms and manages a group from C++:

```cpp
#include <ef/Group.hpp>

ef::Group group;
group.open({ef::DeviceRef::ble("74:A2:35:10:00:19"),
            ef::DeviceRef::ble("60:48:9C:BA:1D:95")});

ef::FormOptions options;          // camera 0 leads
options.lock_timeout_s = 60;
std::string why;
if (group.form(options, &why) != ef::ERROR_CODE::SUCCESS) {
    std::fprintf(stderr, "form failed: %s\n", why.c_str());
}

for (const auto& s : group.sync_status()) {
    // s.mode, s.state, s.theta_ns, s.frame_phase, s.link.key_id
}
```

`group.dissolve()` returns every camera to standalone.
